Cryogenic Insulation
Cryogenic insulation is designed to minimize heat transfer at extremely low temperatures, typically below −150 °C, as used in liquefied gases like nitrogen or LNG. It relies on materials with very low thermal conductivity, such as aerogels, vacuum insulation panels, or multilayer insulation (MLI), to reduce conduction, convection, and radiation heat transfer. Effective cryogenic insulation is critical for reducing boil-off losses, improving energy efficiency, and ensuring safe storage and transport of cryogenic fluids.

Extreme Weather Clothing
Extreme weather clothing is engineered to protect the human body from harsh environmental conditions such as extreme cold, heat, wind, and moisture.
It incorporates advanced materials like aerogel insulation, phase change materials (PCMs), and multilayer systems to regulate heat transfer and maintain thermal comfort. Such clothing is critical for applications in polar expeditions, high-altitude mountaineering, military operations, and cryogenic or industrial environments.

Aerogel materials for biomedical applications
Aerogel materials are highly porous, lightweight structures with large surface area, making them suitable for biomedical applications. They are used in drug delivery, tissue engineering, wound healing, and biosensing due to their tunable properties and biocompatibility. Their unique structure enables controlled release, cell growth support, and efficient interaction with biological systems.

Aerogel Materials for Tactical applications
Aerogels are used in tactical applications for lightweight thermal insulation in extreme environments like deserts and Arctic conditions. They enhance protective gear by improving EMI shielding and reducing heat signatures for infrared stealth. Their ultra-low density and high porosity enable multifunctional performance without adding significant weight.

Aerogel Materials for Electronic applications
Aerogel materials are widely used in electronic applications due to their ultra-low thermal conductivity and lightweight structure. They provide effective thermal management, electrical insulation, batteries, circuits, and electronic devices. Their high porosity and tunable properties enable improved performance, safety, and miniaturization in advanced electronics.
